What if a packet is missing?

In the optional challenge, one packet is missing. Arrange the packets you have, identify the missing sequence number, and explain why the receiver cannot reconstruct the complete message until the missing data is recovered.

Exam knowledge: packet reassembly

1. A message is divided into smaller packets before transmission.

2. Each packet carries part of the message as its payload and may include a sequence number in relevant protocol information.

3. Packets can take different routes and arrive out of order.

4. The receiving device uses sequence information to put the payloads back in the correct order.

5. If a packet is missing or corrupted, reliable communication protocols may arrange for it to be retransmitted before the complete data is accepted.

Remember: Packet formats vary by protocol. Sequence numbering and retransmission are associated with the communication protocols being used; they are not implemented identically by every network protocol.
